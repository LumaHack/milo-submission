Inside an animal cell, and how it divides

Written for testing Milo. Not real course material.
Slide 2: The cell membrane

- Every animal cell is wrapped in a cell membrane.
- It is a phospholipid bilayer: two thin layers of fat.
- It is selectively permeable. We call it the cell's gatekeeper:
  it lets food and oxygen in and lets waste out.
Slide 3: Mitochondria, the battery packs

- In this course we call mitochondria the cell's battery packs.
- They carry out aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen.
- The energy is released as ATP, which the cell spends on everything it does.
- Busy cells, like muscle cells, have hundreds of them.
Slide 4: The nucleus

- The nucleus is the control centre.
- It holds the DNA, packed as chromatin.
- The DNA carries the instructions for making proteins.
- Ribosomes, tiny dots in the cytoplasm, read those instructions and build the proteins.
Slide 5: Lysosomes, the recycling centre

- Lysosomes are small bags full of digestive enzymes.
- They break down worn-out parts of the cell and bacteria the cell has swallowed.
- The useful pieces are recycled to build new parts.
Slide 6: Why cells divide

- Cells divide so the body can grow and repair itself.
- One parent cell becomes two identical daughter cells.
- This kind of division is called mitosis.
Slide 7: Mitosis, step by step

1. The DNA is copied, so each chromosome has two identical halves.
2. The chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell.
3. The two halves are pulled apart to opposite ends of the cell.
4. The cell pinches in the middle and splits into two.
